# Clinical Question Frameworks

Select one primary framework. Do not force every field when it is not meaningful.

## Minimum Research Brief

- Clinical decision or knowledge need
- Population and care setting
- Current practice or comparator
- Primary patient-relevant outcome
- Available data or recruitment pathway
- Time zero and follow-up when longitudinal
- Intended inference: descriptive, causal, diagnostic, prognostic, predictive, or implementation
- Constraints: ethics, privacy, budget, timeline, jurisdiction

## Framework Router

### PICO(T): intervention or randomized question

- **P** Population
- **I** Intervention
- **C** Comparator
- **O** Outcomes, including harms
- **T** Follow-up or assessment time

Write the question so intervention assignment, comparator, primary outcome, and time horizon are unambiguous.

### PECO(T): exposure, etiology, or observational causal question

- **P** Population
- **E** Exposure
- **C** Comparator or counterfactual condition
- **O** Outcome
- **T** Time zero and follow-up

State the target causal contrast and major confounders. Avoid causal verbs if the design cannot support causal inference.

### PIRD: diagnostic accuracy question

- **P** Population and intended-use setting
- **I** Index test
- **R** Reference standard
- **D** Target condition

Define the clinical pathway, positivity thresholds, indeterminate results, and timing between index and reference tests.

### PICOTS: prediction or prognostic model question

- **P** Population
- **I** Intended use or index model
- **C** Comparator model or current practice
- **O** Predicted outcome
- **T** Time horizon
- **S** Setting

Also state time zero, predictor measurement window, intended user, decision threshold, and whether the task is model development, evaluation, updating, or impact assessment.

### SPIDER: qualitative or mixed-methods question

- **S** Sample
- **PI** Phenomenon of interest
- **D** Design
- **E** Evaluation
- **R** Research type

Specify whose perspective is missing and how the findings could change care or implementation.

## Question Quality Check

A final question should be:

- clinically consequential rather than merely statistically convenient;
- answerable with the proposed data and timeframe;
- explicit about population, setting, exposure/intervention/test/model, outcome, and time;
- aligned with one main inferential aim;
- neutral about the expected direction of results;
- narrow enough to support a protocol and analysis plan;
- accompanied by a falsifiable primary hypothesis when appropriate.

Do not call a question novel because it combines two fashionable terms. Require a documented evidence or implementation gap.
