# Evidence and Registration

## Source Routing

Use multiple source classes because no single database establishes novelty.

1. **Current clinical guidance:** WHO, government agencies, recognized specialty societies, NICE, and jurisdiction-specific guidance.
2. **Evidence syntheses:** Cochrane Library and recent high-quality systematic reviews indexed in PubMed.
3. **Primary biomedical studies:** PubMed/MEDLINE and publisher records; use PMC for available full text.
4. **Ongoing and completed studies:** ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP, ISRCTN, EU CTIS, or a jurisdiction-appropriate registry.
5. **Review registrations:** PROSPERO or another domain-appropriate registry.
6. **Citation-network discovery:** OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar; verify metadata and claims against PubMed, Crossref, registry, or publisher records.
7. **Preprints:** Use only as provisional evidence and label non-peer-reviewed status.

When PubMed MCP is available, use it for structured search, metadata retrieval, related-article discovery, and PMC full text. If unavailable, use official PubMed pages or NCBI APIs. Never claim that an abstract-only review verified full-text methods or results.

## Minimum Search Sequence

1. Translate the clinical question into controlled vocabulary and free-text concepts.
2. Search recent guidelines and systematic reviews first.
3. Search primary studies across an appropriate date range without using citation count as a quality filter.
4. Search trial or review registries using condition, intervention/exposure, outcome, and synonyms.
5. Inspect references and cited-by links for pivotal and ongoing work.
6. Update the search immediately before final recommendation when recency matters.

Document databases, exact queries, dates, filters, result counts, and screening logic. Label a rapid scan as a rapid scan; do not present it as a systematic search.

## Evidence Ledger Fields

For every source used to justify a gap, record:

- source type;
- full citation or registry title;
- stable identifier: PMID, DOI, NCT, ISRCTN, PROSPERO, or guideline URL;
- publication or registration status;
- population, design, and setting;
- result or limitation relevant to the candidate idea;
- support level: direct, indirect, conflicting, or background only;
- verification scope: metadata, abstract, methods/results sections, or full text;
- verification date.

## Novelty Gate

Do not write “first,” “novel,” “unexplored,” or “fills a gap” unless all relevant checks are complete:

- a recent systematic review or equivalent evidence map was assessed;
- recent primary studies were searched;
- ongoing and completed registrations were searched;
- population, setting, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, and method differences were compared;
- the remaining gap is clinically meaningful rather than a trivial combination of variables;
- stable identifiers were verified.

Classify the gap as one or more of:

- evidence gap;
- conflicting evidence;
- external-validity or equity gap;
- diagnostic, prognostic, or calibration gap;
- comparative-effectiveness gap;
- implementation gap;
- safety or harms gap;
- measurement or outcome gap;
- methods or reproducibility gap.

If verification is incomplete, write “candidate gap pending verification.”

## Clinical Trial and Publication Requirements

Check the current ICMJE recommendations and local law or policy. Start from:

- ICMJE clinical trial registration and data sharing: https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/publishing-and-editorial-issues/clinical-trial-registration.html
- ClinicalTrials.gov: https://clinicaltrials.gov/
- WHO ICTRP: https://www.who.int/clinical-trials-registry-platform

Do not treat registry presence as proof that a study is scientifically valid, ethically approved, completed, or accurately reported.
